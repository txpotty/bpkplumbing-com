    Residential expansion tank installs usually cost $250 to $550 with the tank and labor. Attic installs, larger tanks or a new PRV can bring it to $600 to $1,000.

    Heated water expands. On a closed system it has nowhere to go, so pressure climbs every cycle and wears out heater components and relief valves, sometimes causing leaks, while the expansion tank gives that water an air cushion to push against instead. The air cushion absorbs it.

    Water expands when it heats, and on a closed system that extra volume raises the pressure in every pipe. Each heating cycle spikes the pressure when there is no tank, which wears out the heater, the fixtures and the relief valve in Layton.     Closed plumbing systems need somewhere to put expanded hot water. Without a tank, thermal expansion can damage the heater, pipes and fixtures, leading to leaks and early failure, and plenty of local codes in Utah require a tank for that reason. The tank itself is small.

    A dripping T and P valve on the water heater is the most common symptom. Pressure that swings up and down is another, and a waterlogged tank sounds dull all the way up when you tap it and feels heavy, which means the diaphragm has failed. At that point it gets replaced.
