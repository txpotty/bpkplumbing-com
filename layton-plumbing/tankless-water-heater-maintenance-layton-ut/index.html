    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Maintenance in Layton

    The cost for tankless water heater maintenance in Layton, UT typically ranges from $150 to $300, depending on the unit's age, condition and the extent of descaling required. This includes a full flush and inspection of critical components. More complex issues or additional repairs would be quoted on their own.

    Neglecting maintenance can lead to several problems, including reduced heating efficiency, higher utility bills, decreased hot water output and eventually, costly breakdowns. Mineral buildup can seriously restrict water flow and damage the heat exchanger, potentially leading to premature unit failure and expensive repairs or replacement.

    BPK Plumbing services all major brands of tankless water heaters, including Rinnai, Noritz, Navien, Bosch, State, Rheem and more. Our technicians are trained on the specific maintenance requirements and diagnostics for a wide range of models to provide expert care for your unit.

    Common signs include reduced hot water flow, fluctuating water temperatures, strange noises coming from the unit, or error codes displayed on the control panel. If you notice any of these, it's a strong indication that your tankless water heater in Layton is due for professional maintenance.

    The descaling process involves shutting off the unit, isolating the water lines and connecting a circulating pump to flush a mild acidic solution through the heat exchanger. This solution dissolves mineral deposits like calcium and magnesium, which are common in hard water, restoring heat transfer efficiency.

    Most visits take one to two hours, including the descaling cycle. Installing isolation valves on the same trip adds about an hour.

    Rinnai shows LC codes, Navien may show E003 or E030 and Noritz often shows 90 or 99 when scale or airflow is a problem. These codes typically clear after proper cleaning.

    Most tankless water heater manufacturers recommend annual maintenance to support good performance and extend the unit's lifespan. In areas with harder water, more frequent checks, perhaps every 6 to 9 months, might help to prevent excessive scale buildup.
