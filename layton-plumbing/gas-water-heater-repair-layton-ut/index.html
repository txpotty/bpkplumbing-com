    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Layton

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. The usual failures are the blower motor, the pressure switch and the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. The control's error code is our starting point. Then each part gets a meter test.

    Flammable Vapor Sensor Repair in Layton

    Since about 2003, most residential gas tanks have been built with a flammable vapor ignition resistant combustion chamber. A flame arrestor screen at the base lets air in and keeps flame from getting out. When lint, dust or pet hair coats that screen, the burner starves, the chamber overheats and a thermal switch shuts the whole heater down, sometimes several times a week. We clean the screen and the chamber. A tripped thermal switch gets replaced.

    Pilot Light Repair in Layton

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. With a millivolt meter, it takes about a minute to see if it is putting out enough. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Most of these jobs run about an hour.

    Repairing Older Gas Water Heaters in Layton

    Older tanks with standing pilots and no vapor sensor are still out there in Layton, mostly in basements and utility closets. Parts for them are simpler, and a universal thermocouple fits most.     Venting and Draft Repair in Layton, UT

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot.
