    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Layton

    A trench through a paver driveway or a mature garden can cost more to restore than the pipe itself. That is where trenchless pays off.     PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Layton

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. PEX usually fails at a fitting. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, while PEX gets a fresh crimp ring or expansion fitting with the right tool for the brand.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Layton

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Older homes in Layton may still have galvanized steel supply lines that rust inside and cause low pressure and rusty water. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. We price each one for you.

    Main Water Line Repair in Layton

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. A leak here shows up as a soggy strip of lawn and a meter dial that never rests. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. A moving dial with the house valve closed means an underground leak. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, so the crew opens one small pit rather than trenching the whole run.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Layton

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. It works best on straight runs. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, and a length of HDPE trails behind it in the same path. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Type K copper, burial-rated PEX or HDPE poly. All three work well. Copper resists punctures and lasts decades, PEX flexes with the soil and HDPE is what we use for trenchless pulls. We recommend one after looking at your soil, line length and budget.

    Labor is warrantied, always. New pipe and fittings carry the manufacturer warranty as well, with the coverage listed on your quote before we begin. Keep the invoice. It is your proof of coverage.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Resulting damage sometimes is.     Small spot repairs often do not need one. Full replacements usually do. Rules vary by jurisdiction in Layton, so BPK Plumbing checks the local requirement for your address and pulls the permit when one applies. An inspection happens with the pipe still exposed.

    Age is the biggest cause. Old pipe corrodes. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, plus accidental hits from fence posts, irrigation trenching and utility work. Poor original installation is another, such as pipe laid on sharp rock.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, a bill that keeps rising and rusty or cloudy water can all point to a damaged line. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.
