    Under-Sink Grease Trap Maintenance in Layton, UT

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, usually 10 to 50 gallons per minute, and they hold far less than people expect. In a busy kitchen, that means cleaning every week or two. We take the lid off, remove grease and solids, scrape the baffles, check the flow control fitting on the inlet, and replace the lid gasket if it is flattened or torn. Most odor from these units comes from a bad gasket.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance in Layton

    Pumping frequency comes from two numbers: how big the trap is and how fast your kitchen fills it. The first few visits, we measure fill rate with a sludge judge. From there we set a recurring date, and the truck shows up without you having to call the office and remind anyone. Schedules shift if your menu does.

    Grease Trap Cleaning Cost in Layton, UT

    Cost follows trap size and fill level. Small under-sink traps go quickly.     Commercial Grease Trap Pumping in Layton

    Fryer-heavy menus load a trap fastest. Two weeks is enough for a wings place to fill a 50-gallon trap. The trucks have the hose length for traps behind walk-ins or in basements, plus tank room for interceptors over 1,000 gallons. We pump every one to the floor.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Cleaning in Layton

    Gravity interceptors in the parking lot often hold 750 to 2,000 gallons across two or three compartments. Each compartment gets pumped separately. A hard grease cap gets broken up with a pressure wand before pumping, otherwise it plugs the suction hose and half of it stays in the tank. No one goes inside without confined-space gear.

    Grease Line Jetting in Layton

    Grease that slips past a full trap coats the 4-inch line out to the main. It builds up in rings, thickest at the bottom of the pipe. Snaking just bores a small path that closes up in weeks, but a rotating jetter nozzle strips grease off the full wall of the pipe. We run a camera after to confirm.

    Grease Trap Hydro-Jetting in Layton, UT

    Some traps have years of grease baked onto the walls and inlet piping. Pumping alone leaves that crust in place. We jet the inside of the empty trap and the line feeding it, which takes the unit back to bare walls and restores the capacity the crust was eating up. It adds about an hour.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance Plans in Layton

    Most pretreatment programs use the 25 percent rule: pump before floating grease and settled solids together take up a quarter of the trap's liquid depth. Other programs simply set a fixed interval, commonly 90 days for large interceptors. We work to whichever rule applies. Changes are easy.
