    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Replacement in Layton, UT

    Orangeburg is tar-soaked wood fiber. It was installed in a lot of homes in the decades after the war, and over time it softens, blisters and squashes into an oval that catches everything going by. Clay holds up better, except at the joints. When the camera shows trouble in three or more places, a full replacement is usually cheaper than chasing spot repairs.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Layton

    A collapsed run or a long belly usually means we dig. The crew trenches the full length of the lateral, removes the failed pipe and sets new PVC on compacted bedding with steady fall. Sod gets cut and rolled aside first when the lawn allows it. The inspector sees open pipe. Backfill goes in compacted lifts so the trench line does not sink into a ditch a year later.

    Sewer Codes and Inspections in Layton, UT

    Inspectors check what gets buried. Utah adopts the International Plumbing Code with state amendments, and plumbers must be licensed by the state to do the work. On a sewer job that means pipe size, minimum fall, bedding material, cleanout locations and the connection at the main or the tap. We book the inspection and meet the inspector. Unpermitted pipe sometimes has to be uncovered again for inspection, at the owner's expense.

    Spot Sewer Repairs in Layton, UT

    Sometimes one joint is the whole problem. With only one bad section, we excavate a single small pit and splice in new PVC using shielded couplings. That costs much less. We only suggest it when the footage backs it up.

    Emergency Sewer Collapse Repair in Layton

    Every drain in the house stops when a lateral collapses. The tech scopes the line, marks the break and lets you know whether the dig can start next day or has to wait on locates. Utility marking takes a few days in most areas. We can often set up a bypass so one bathroom stays usable.

    Sewer Pipe Materials We Install in Layton, UT

    PVC covers most of our trenched work. SDR 35 is the common sewer grade for buried laterals, Schedule 40 shows up where code or the inspector wants a heavier wall, and HDPE is our pick for bursting. Rocky ground can make sewer line and water service repairs more involved, and trenchless methods are often a good fit where digging is tough. Cast iron still has a place inside some buildings. We will go over which one fits your depth, soil and budget.

    Trenchless Sewer Replacement in Layton, UT

    Two holes instead of one long trench. Pipe bursting drags a cone-shaped head through the old line, cracking it outward while new fused HDPE follows right behind. The other option is a cured-in-place liner. Lining puts a resin tube inside the old pipe and cures it hard, but only if the host pipe is still mostly round and has no major sags.

    Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Layton

    Cast iron rots from the inside out. The bottom of a cast iron pipe goes first, and the camera will show a trench rusted through even when the top looks fine. Rough scale catches everything. We swap the outdoor section for plastic pipe. For cast iron under a slab, we talk through lining versus rerouting.
