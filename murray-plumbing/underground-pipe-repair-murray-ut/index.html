    Galvanized and Polybutylene Line Replacement in Murray

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside until little water gets through. Polybutylene splits instead. If your buried service is either one, patches are usually temporary, which is why we recommend a full swap to PEX or copper threaded along the existing route. You will notice the pressure.

    Polybutylene Pipe Replacement in Murray

    Polybutylene was used in a lot of homes built in the 1980s and early 1990s. It fails without warning. The gray plastic turns brittle as chlorine in the water attacks it, and buried runs tend to split at fittings or where the pipe was bent tight during installation. Patching it just moves the next leak. We use PEX or copper.

    Trenchless Underground Pipe Repair in Murray

    Digging a trench across a mature yard is expensive to restore. Trenchless avoids most of it. We open small access pits at each end, then pull new pipe through the old path or line the old pipe in place, which saves sod, irrigation, pavers and concrete that would otherwise have to be torn out and replaced. Faster, too.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Underground Pipe Repair in Murray

    A single repair on a buried water line typically costs $800 to $2,500 including locating and excavation. A new service runs higher. Main water service replacement typically runs $2,500 to $6,500, and sewer lateral repairs range from $1,500 to $5,000 depending on how deep the pipe sits. We write it up before digging.

    Water lines get PEX or Type K copper. Each is approved for direct bury. Sewer lines get Schedule 40 PVC, and the final choice depends on the existing pipe, the soil and what your local code allows. Couplings get sized to both pipes.

    Acoustic ground microphones pick up the sound of escaping water. We also use tracer gas. Hydrogen-nitrogen gas pushed into the line rises through the soil at the leak, and electronic locators trace the exact pipe route so we know where to listen. Together they narrow it to a few feet.

    It gets worse and more expensive. Water erodes the ground. A leaking service can undermine a driveway, soak a foundation, grow mold in a crawlspace and run up the water bill every month, and a cracked sewer lateral can let sewage into the soil around your home. Fix it sooner.

    In most cities, the homeowner owns that line. The utility owns the meter. The utility usually maintains the meter and the pipe on the street side, so we check which side the leak is on first. It matters for the bill.

    Shallow irrigation leaks are sometimes a fair DIY job. Service lines are different. Main water lines and sewer laterals require locating, utility marks and code-compliant fittings rated for burial, so those should go to a licensed plumber. Utilities have to be marked before digging.
