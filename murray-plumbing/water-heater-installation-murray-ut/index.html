    Tank and Tankless Water Heaters in Murray, UT

    Gas Tank Water Heater Installation in Murray

    A gas tank is the most common water heater we install, in 40, 50 and 75 gallon sizes. Every job gets a fresh connector, a drip leg, a shutoff and a vent connector with the right rise toward the chimney or flue. We test draft before leaving. Big families do well with gas.

    Electric Water Heater Installation in Murray, UT

    Electric tanks need a 240-volt circuit, usually a 30-amp double-pole breaker with 10-gauge wire. We confirm the breaker and wire size match the new unit before connecting it, since many older homes have the wrong gauge. No venting needed. You get more spots to put it.

    Water Heater Installation Cost in Murray, UT

    Up front, a tank heater costs less than tankless. Gas tankless installs often run higher because of the bigger gas line and new venting, and attic jobs, relocations or code upgrades add labor on top of that. Your quote lists permits. We compare operating costs so you can choose with real numbers.

    Hybrid Heat Pump Water Heater Installation in Murray, UT

    Heat pump water heaters pull warmth from the surrounding air and can use about a third of the power of a standard electric tank. They need roughly 700 cubic feet of air around them and a condensate drain, so a warm garage or a large utility room usually works better than a closet. Rebates may apply. We will tell you if your space works.

    Water Heater Expansion Tank Installation in Murray

    Closed plumbing systems trap the extra volume water gains when it heats. Backflow prevention is taken seriously on irrigation systems here, and BPK Plumbing can test and repair assemblies to meet local requirements. An expansion tank gives that water somewhere to go, so the relief valve stops dripping and pipe joints stop taking pressure spikes every time the burner runs. We pre-charge it to your house pressure. It mounts on the cold inlet.

    Expansion Tank Installation in Murray, UT

    A pressure reducing valve or check valve on the main turns your plumbing into a closed system. When the water heats and expands with nowhere to go, pressure climbs and stresses the relief valve, the toilet fill valves and every supply line in the house. An expansion tank absorbs it. We pre-charge it to match your water pressure.

    Electric Tank Water Heater Installation in Murray, UT

    Without gas service, an electric tank is the usual pick. We match voltage and breaker size to the panel, and since there is no vent, an electric tank can sit in spots a gas unit cannot. A drain pan goes under closet installs. Quiet and simple.

    Tankless Water Heater Installation in Murray, UT

    Going tankless means the heater fires only when a tap opens, and it never runs out. Gas tankless models usually need a bigger gas line than the old tank had, plus a dedicated vent run through the wall or roof. Upfront cost is higher. We check gas capacity before quoting.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Murray

    Most installs in Murray, UT land between $1,200 and $3,500. Where yours falls depends on tank or tankless, capacity, fuel type and any plumbing or electrical changes the job needs. Hard access costs more. We give you the figure before work starts.

    Household size and peak demand decide the size. Most homes with two to four people use a 40 to 50 gallon tank, and larger households move up to 60 to 80 gallons. Tankless is sized by GPM.

    Rusty hot water, rumbling from the tank, water at the base and an age past ten years are the big ones. Short hot water often points to heavy sediment or a broken dip tube. We can inspect it.

    A standard tank replacement typically runs two to four hours. Tankless installs or relocations can take most of a day.

    Our crews install Rheem, AO Smith, Bradford White, Navien and Rinnai, all brands with solid parts support and decent tank warranties. We recommend based on price and use.

    A tank heater usually lasts 8 to 12 years with maintenance. Tankless units often go 15 to 20 years because they do not sit full of hot water and their parts can be replaced.

    Doing it yourself means handling gas, 240-volt power and pressurized water at once. A bad vent joint or a missing relief line can lead to carbon monoxide, scalding or a tank failure, and many local codes require a licensed plumber anyway. Hire a pro.
