    Pilot Light and Ignition Repair in Sandy

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. The tech pulls the pilot assembly and checks the thermocouple tip, the orifice and the igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Most trucks carry the common pilot kits.

    Burner Cleaning and Repair in Sandy, UT

    Yellow flame tips, soot on the burner door or a roaring sound at startup all mean the burner is not getting clean air. We pull the burner, brush the ports and blow out the orifice. We also confirm the orifice is drilled for the fuel you have, because a natural gas orifice on a propane heater, or the reverse, will soot up the flue and push carbon monoxide levels well past normal. A good flame is blue and steady. Rust flakes on the burner send us to check the flue baffle next.

    Repairing Older Gas Water Heaters in Sandy

    Older tanks with standing pilots and no vapor sensor are still out there in Sandy, mostly in basements and utility closets. Their parts are simple, and a universal thermocouple fits nearly all of them. We can keep a pre-2003 heater running when the tank is sound, but we also check the draft hood, the flue connector and the gas cock, since those fittings on an old install are often original and corroded. Some old setups lack a sediment trap. One goes in while we are there.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Sandy

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. Propane units need a matching orifice and a control set near 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.

    Repair or Replace: Straight Answers in Sandy, UT

    Before any wrench turns, you get a written price for the repair and an honest read on the tank in your Sandy, UT home. A bad thermocouple on a six-year-old tank is an easy repair to justify. A fourteen-year-old tank with a rusty flue, a leaking bottom and a failed gas valve is a different story, and putting a $500 control on it rarely makes sense.
