    What Goes Into a Tankless Install in Sandy, UT

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Sandy, UT

    Calcium scale is the most common reason tankless heaters fail. Water softeners are a common fixture in Utah homes, and many newer houses are built with a softener loop already in place. Scale coats the heat exchanger, cuts efficiency and triggers error codes, and left alone it can crack the exchanger and end the warranty claim before it starts. Flush it yearly. Service valves make it easy.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Services in Sandy, UT

    Businesses in Sandy, UT with steady hot water use, like salons, restaurants and small gyms, often get more out of two or three tankless units racked together than one big tank. One unit can fail and the rest still carry the load. We size the rack by peak demand. Expect a bigger gas line and a mechanical permit on commercial jobs.

    Rinnai, Navien and Noritz Installation in Sandy, UT

    We install Rinnai, Navien, Noritz and Rheem in Sandy, UT homes all year long. Rinnai brings range and parts support, Navien offers built-in recirc, Noritz makes compact condensing units for tight spots, and Rheem rounds out the list. Warranty terms vary. We explain the differences.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Sandy, UT

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Sandy, UT homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. Whole-house electric units pull a lot of current. Some models need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, more than a 100-amp panel can usually spare alongside the range and AC. Panel capacity gets checked up front.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Sandy, UT

    Switching from a tank takes more than unbolting one and hanging the other. A 40-gallon tank burns about 40,000 BTU, but a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the meter line nearly always gets bigger. Venting changes too. Both are on the estimate.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Sandy

    Conversions in Sandy usually take a full day. The old tank gets drained and hauled off, the gas line is upsized and tested, a new vent goes out through a wall or roof, and the unit is mounted, plumbed with isolation valves and fired. You shower that night. The inspector comes after.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Sandy

    Propane homes in Sandy can run tankless units too, using a model built or converted for propane. Since propane runs at different pressure from natural gas, the regulator, line sizing and gas valve must all match, and the supplier may need to swap the tank regulator. Tank size matters too.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Sandy

    Gas tankless units in Sandy are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. Gas is the catch. In most houses, the line from the meter has to be upsized, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or bigger. We size it and pressure test it before the unit is lit.
