    Well Pump Efficiency Upgrades in Sandy, UT

    Older 1 HP pumps running against a 60 psi switch use more power than most people expect, especially on a well with a long horizontal run to the house. A correctly sized pump, or a variable-speed drive, can cut the run time. Not always worth it. We will show you the payback.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Sandy

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Most fail from sand chewing up the impellers or a lightning surge that cooks the windings. We megger the motor before we pull it. That tells us whether the problem is down the hole or up top.

    Pump and Pressure Tank Matching in Sandy

    Pump and tank work as a pair. An undersized or waterlogged tank forces dozens of extra starts a day, and each start puts heat into the motor windings. We check it on every pump job.

    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Sandy

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes a healthy pump look sick. If the pump clicks on every time someone runs a faucet for ten seconds, the bladder in the tank has probably failed and the pump is short cycling itself to death. We check the air charge with a gauge. The air charge should read 2 psi under the switch cut-in.

    Sandy Wells and Submersible Pumps in Sandy

    Sand kills submersibles early. A well that pumps sand will wear impellers down in a few years, so we check for grit in the old pump and talk about setting the new one higher off the bottom or adding a sand separator. Where the pump hangs matters.

    Control Box and Pressure Switch Replacement in Sandy, UT

    Not every dry faucet means a dead pump. A pressure switch with pitted contacts or a plugged sensing tube can stop the pump even though nothing is wrong down the well. A new switch runs about $30 in parts. You get to see the old part.

    Jet Pump Replacement in Sandy, UT

    Jet pumps sit above ground, in a well house or basement, and pull water up by suction from wells where the water level is within about 25 feet. Deep-well jets with a two-pipe setup can reach further, but they work harder. The common failure is losing prime. Usually a leaking foot valve or a cracked suction line lets the water fall back down the well, so we check both before we blame the pump.

    Well Pump Sizing in Sandy

    Sizing starts with the well log. The log, or a sounder down the casing, gives us static water level and well depth, and a draw-down test tells us roughly how many gallons per minute the well can actually give. Pumps bigger than the well can supply run dry.
