    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Sandy

    Power outages during heavy storms can leave a primary sump pump useless, leading to basement flooding. A battery backup sump pump provides vital protection when the power goes out. We install dependable backup systems that activate automatically, keeping water moving even without electricity. This is an important safeguard for homes in Sandy that lose power during storms, offering peace of mind in severe weather. We tie these systems seamlessly with your existing plumbing.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Sandy

    Pedestal sump pumps are an excellent choice for narrow or shallow sump pits, as their motor sits above the water level. This design makes them easier to maintain and can often offer a longer lifespan because the motor stays out of the water. BPK Plumbing can properly install pedestal sump pumps in your Sandy home, with the right sizing and setup for effective water removal without crowding your pit.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Sandy

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by locating the low point where water collects. We saw cut the slab, excavate a pit roughly 18 to 24 inches wide and 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a perforated or solid liner depending on whether we are collecting groundwater or surface seepage. Gravel goes around the liner to help water flow in while keeping fine soil out. We then patch the slab flush with new concrete so the floor stays clean and level.

    Commercial and Multi-Unit Sump Installations in Sandy

    Office buildings, parking structures and multi-unit properties with below-grade levels usually need duplex sump systems with two pumps and an alternating control panel. The panel switches lead and lag pumps on each cycle so wear stays even, and a high water alarm alerts building staff. We coordinate electrical with your electrician and follow manufacturer specs for basin size and float settings. Property managers get a simple maintenance schedule to keep both pumps exercised.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Sandy

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit typically runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab generally falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    Watch for a pump that runs constantly, cycles on and off rapidly, makes grinding noises or never seems to turn on. Rust stains, a stuck float and water sitting above the switch level are also warning signs.

    A standard basement sump pump installation typically takes 4 to 8 hours. If a new sump pit needs to be excavated, or there are unexpected challenges with plumbing or electrical access, the job might stretch to a full day. Our team works efficiently to limit disruption to your home.

    Replacing a pump generally does not need a permit, but new pits, new discharge routes or new electrical circuits may. We check local requirements and pull permits when they apply.

    Spring snowmelt can raise groundwater and creek levels in Utah, sometimes pushing water into basements that stay dry the rest of the year. Because storms and power outages tend to arrive together, a battery backup sump pump is strongly recommended. It provides crucial protection by switching on automatically when the main power fails, so your basement stays dry even without electricity. It is a smart investment for peace of mind.

    Most residential basements need a 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump, but the right answer depends on how fast water enters the pit and how high it must be lifted. We measure both before recommending a model.

    Usually, standard home insurance policies do not cover a new sump pump installation or repairs unless the damage was caused by a sudden, accidental event like a burst pipe, rather than gradual wear and tear or groundwater seepage. Some policies offer separate water backup or sump pump failure endorsements for an extra premium. It is best to check with your insurance provider.
