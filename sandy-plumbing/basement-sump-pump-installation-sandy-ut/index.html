    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Sandy

    Figure on roughly $800 to $2,000 for most basement sump pump installs in Sandy. At the low end you get a pump swap in a pit that is already there, while a new pit cut through the slab, a long discharge run, or a battery backup pushes the total toward the top of that range. We quote after seeing it.

    For some homeowners, a same-model pump swap is a fair weekend job. New pits and backup systems are a different matter, because those mistakes end in flooding, cracked slabs or electrical hazards, and fixing them costs more than paying for the install up front. Leave those to us.

    Mostly cast iron pumps from Zoeller, Wayne, Liberty Pumps and Little Giant. Parts for all four are easy to find at supply houses, which matters when a switch fails on a Sunday and you want the pump running again without waiting a week for an order to ship. Common models ride on our trucks.

    Submersibles sit in the pit, sealed, and run quiet. A pedestal mounts the motor on a column above the water, which makes them cheaper and easier to service but louder, so they work best in an unfinished utility room rather than a finished family room. Either type gets the job done.

    We strongly recommend professional installation. The job combines electrical, plumbing and concrete work, and a bad seal on the lid, an undersized pit or a discharge that drains toward the house can leave you worse off than having no pump at all. Mistakes show up in storms.

    Plan on 4 to 8 hours for a standard install. Digging a new pit and patching the slab can take the whole day, and tricky electrical access or a long discharge run through a finished wall adds time on top of that. You get a time estimate first.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. A sticking float or rust streaks on the housing also mean trouble, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Do not wait for the storm.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Corrosion on the housing and a float that hangs up are warning signs, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Over seven years old is suspect.
