    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Maintenance in Sandy

    The cost for tankless water heater maintenance in Sandy, UT generally ranges from $150 to $300, depending on the unit's age, condition and the extent of descaling required. This includes a full flush and inspection of critical components. More complex issues or additional repairs would be quoted on their own.

    The descaling process involves shutting off the unit, isolating the water lines and connecting a circulating pump to flush a specialized acidic solution through the heat exchanger. This solution dissolves mineral deposits like calcium and magnesium, which are common in hard water, restoring heat transfer efficiency.

    While some basic tasks like cleaning external filters might be possible for a homeowner, full maintenance, especially descaling the heat exchanger and inspecting gas lines, should be left to a licensed professional. Improper servicing can damage the unit, void warranties and create safety risks related to gas and carbon monoxide.

    Most tankless water heater manufacturers recommend annual maintenance to ensure good performance and extend the unit's lifespan. In areas with harder water, more frequent checks, perhaps every 6 to 9 months, might help to prevent excessive scale buildup.

    Many tankless water heater manufacturers require proof of regular, professional maintenance to keep your unit's warranty valid. Skipping annual service can cancel the warranty, leaving you responsible for costly repairs that would otherwise be covered. Always check your specific warranty terms.

    Once a year is standard, and homes with very hard water and no softener may need it every six to nine months. Heavy users and commercial units need it more often.

    Neglecting maintenance can lead to several problems, including reduced heating efficiency, higher utility bills, decreased hot water output and eventually, costly breakdowns. Mineral buildup can severely restrict water flow and damage the heat exchanger, potentially leading to premature unit failure and expensive repairs or replacement.

Descaling removes mineral deposits that force the unit to work harder, using more energy to heat water.
