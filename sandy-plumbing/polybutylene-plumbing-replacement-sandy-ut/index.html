    Polybutylene Identification and Inspection in Sandy

    Polybutylene is usually gray, though it can be white, blue or black, and is often stamped PB2110. You may see it at the water heater, under sinks or near the main shutoff.     When a home with polybutylene pipes sits on a slab foundation, repiping becomes a more complex process. PEX is an excellent choice for Utah homes, as it resists freezing better than rigid pipes and expands to accommodate temperature changes. We carefully plan the repipe to minimize disruption, often running new lines through attics or existing wall cavities, ensuring a complete and long-lasting upgrade for your entire plumbing system.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Sandy

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Properly installed PEX piping typically has a lifespan of 25 to 50 years, while copper piping can last 50 to 70 years or even longer. Both are significant upgrades from polybutylene and offer decades of reliable service for your Sandy, UT home.

    While there will be some disruption, BPK Plumbing strives to limit it. We take precautions to protect your property, cover furniture, and clean up completely. We often complete the work in sections to maintain some water access during the project.

    Common signs include frequent leaks, discolored water, low water pressure, or visible grey or blue plastic pipes often marked 'PB2110'. If your home was built between 1978 and 1995 and has these pipes, replacement is strongly advised.

    Both PEX and copper are strong choices. PEX is flexible, less expensive, more freeze-resistant, and handles ground movement better than rigid pipe. Copper offers superior rigidity, a long lifespan, and a strong track record. The 'better' choice depends on your budget, preferences, and home's specific needs.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A and Type L copper with Viega ProPress or soldered fittings. We recommend materials based on your home and water conditions.

    Removing and replacing an entire supply system requires routing, pressure testing and permits. A licensed crew finishes faster and provides warranties and documentation.

    Utah water is among the hardest in the country in many areas, and scale builds up quickly in water heaters, faucets and appliances.
