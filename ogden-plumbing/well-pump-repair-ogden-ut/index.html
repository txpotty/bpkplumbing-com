    Jet Pump Repair in Ogden, UT

    Shallow wells and booster systems often use jet pumps. Common problems are loss of prime, a leaking shaft seal and a worn impeller. Our crew in Ogden restores prime, replaces worn parts and checks the suction line for leaks that let air in. Where water levels swing through the year, a falling level can starve the pump and cost it prime.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Ogden

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. Usually the bladder has failed or the tank has lost its air charge. We check the precharge with the tank drained. Set it 2 psi under cut-in, which means about 38 psi for a 40/60 switch.

    Deep Well Pump Repair in Ogden

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up, run quietly and last, but motors, impellers and splices still wear out. We pull the pump with the right equipment, test it and replace only what failed. Heat-shrink splice kits and a torque arrestor protect the new work in your Ogden, UT well.

    Jet Pump System Repair in Ogden, UT

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Most failures start with an air leak somewhere in the suction line. BPK Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and watches it reach cut-out pressure. A jet pump that runs nonstop without reaching pressure has almost always lost prime or sucked air through a cracked fitting.

    Hot weather makes a dry house worse. No-water calls get a fast response and, when parts allow, a same-day fix. A temporary setup can sometimes cover the house while a pump is on order. While the truck is on the way, the dispatcher can help you check the breaker and switch.

    Low Water Pressure Repair in Ogden

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. Common causes include a plugged sediment filter, a low switch setting or a yard line leak. In some cases the pump is fine and the well just cannot keep up with demand. We test each one.

    Control Box & Electrical Repair in Ogden, UT

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. Breaker trips usually come first. With the power locked out, we test and replace the failed parts and tighten every terminal. Wiring meets Utah code before we restart the pump.

    Well Pump Diagnostic Services in Ogden, UT

    Voltage, amperage, pressure and flow rate tell us most of what is happening down the well without pulling a single length of pipe. A weak motor draws high amps. A clogged intake or a dropping water level shows up as low flow with normal amps, and that changes the repair entirely. Testing first saves a pull.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Repair in Ogden

    Expect $300 for a pressure switch and up to $2,000 or more for a submersible motor replacement. Well depth changes the number most. A deep well means more drop pipe and wire to pull and replace, plus more labor time on the hoist, so the same pump costs more to swap in a deeper hole.

    Submersible pumps commonly last 8 to 15 years, depending on run time, sand and water quality. Short cycling from a bad pressure tank can cut that down a lot.

    Pulling a pump means heavy pipe, high voltage and the risk of contaminating the well. Most homeowners are better off calling a pro with a hoist.

    Simple fixes like a pressure switch take a few hours at most. A submersible swap can run all day if the well is deep.

    Start with the switch and the filter. A clogged filter or a low cut-in setting causes a lot of weak pressure complaints. If those check out, a larger pressure tank, a constant pressure system or a booster pump may be the answer, and we can tell you which fits your well's yield.

    We work with Franklin Electric, Grundfos, Goulds, Flint and Walling and similar brands. Pressure tanks from Well-X-Trol, Amtrol and Flexcon are on the list too.

    Like-for-like pump replacement usually does not require a permit, but deepening or modifying the well falls under state well rules.
