    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Ogden

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Ogden

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by finding the low point where water collects. We saw cut the slab, excavate a pit roughly 18 to 24 inches wide and 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a perforated or solid liner depending on whether we are collecting groundwater or surface seepage. Gravel goes around the liner to help water flow in while keeping fine soil out. We then patch the slab flush with new concrete so the floor stays clean and level.

    Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Ogden

    A great pump is useless if the water it moves runs right back toward the foundation. We route rigid PVC discharge pipe out of the basement and extend it at least 10 feet from the house or into a pop-up emitter or dry well. A spring or swing check valve keeps the column of water from dropping back into the pit after every cycle, which protects the motor. Freezing temperatures are a regular part of Utah winters, and pipes in garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls can freeze during cold stretches. Where lines can freeze, we add a freeze-resistant outlet so ice cannot block the line.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Ogden, UT

    Submersible sump pumps are designed to sit directly in the sump pit, submerged in water, which makes them quieter and less intrusive. They are powerful and effective for basements that take on a lot of water. Our team in Ogden, UT will install your submersible pump with precision, ensuring proper sealing, discharge piping and electrical connections for quiet, reliable operation, which matters most in finished basements.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Ogden

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit typically runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab usually falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit typically takes two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and concrete patching typically takes most of a day.

    Typically, standard home insurance policies do not cover a new sump pump installation or repairs unless the damage was caused by a sudden, accidental event like a burst pipe, rather than gradual wear and tear or groundwater seepage. Some policies offer separate water backup or sump pump failure endorsements for an extra premium. It is best to check with your insurance provider.

    Manufacturer warranties typically range from 3 to 5 years on quality cast iron pumps. We also back our installation labor, and we will walk you through the exact coverage for the model you choose.

    Spring snowmelt can raise groundwater and creek levels in Utah, sometimes pushing water into basements that stay dry the rest of the year. Because storms and power outages tend to arrive together, a battery backup sump pump is highly recommended. It provides crucial protection by switching on automatically when the main power fails, so your basement stays dry even without electricity. It is a wise investment for peace of mind.

    In many areas, including Ogden, a plumbing permit may be required for new sump pump installations or major replacements to confirm compliance with local building codes. BPK Plumbing will help you work through the permit process and make sure all work is up to code.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, designed to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for maximum protection.

    Most residential basements need a 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump, but the right answer depends on how fast water enters the pit and how high it must be lifted. We measure both before recommending a model.
