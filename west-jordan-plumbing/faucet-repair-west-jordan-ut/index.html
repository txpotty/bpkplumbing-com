    Low Water Flow Faucet Repair in West Jordan

    Sputtering flow, where the water spits and surges, often means air or debris in the line after a water main repair, or a partly clogged cartridge screen. Flushing usually clears it. On pull-down faucets, the check valves in the hose can stick too, and we clean or replace those.

    Tub Spout and Diverter Repair in West Jordan, UT

    Water still running from the tub spout while the shower is on means the diverter is worn, either the gate inside the spout or a diverter stem in the valve. There are slip-on and threaded spouts.     Compression Faucet Repair in West Jordan

    Two-handle faucets with separate hot and cold handles are often compression faucets, which seal by pressing a rubber washer down onto a brass seat inside the valve. Over time the washer hardens and the seat wears rough. We replace the washer and dress or replace the seat with a seat wrench, and the packing gets redone if the stem leaks.

    Dripping Faucet Solutions in West Jordan

    Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Repair in West Jordan

    Most faucet repairs in West Jordan run $120 to $350, depending on the valve type and the parts, with a washer job at the low end and a high-end cartridge at the top. Sensor faucets sometimes run higher. You get a written price before any work.

    Delta, Moen, Kohler, American Standard and Pfister cover most of what we see in Utah homes, and we stock their common cartridges. Other brands are fine too.

    We do, including Grohe, Hansgrohe, Brizo and Kohler's higher lines, which often use metric cartridges you will not find at a hardware store. A few of these need a special order. We confirm the cartridge before the visit when we can.

    It can. A faucet dripping once a second loses over 3,000 gallons a year, which is roughly the water used in 180 showers and enough to notice on the bill. Replacing the washer or cartridge ends it.

    Watch for a drip that gets faster, green or white crust at the base, a handle that takes more force to close, and water in the cabinet below. Any of these will get worse. Catching them early keeps the repair to a cartridge.

    Utah water is among the hardest in the country in many areas, and scale builds up quickly in water heaters, faucets and appliances. Calcium and magnesium in hard water build up inside cartridges and aerators, stiffen moving parts and scratch the seals until the faucet drips. Flow drops too. An aerator cleaning every few months slows it down.

    Low pressure at a single faucet is almost always the aerator or the cartridge. Low pressure everywhere could be the regulator, the supply or old pipes.

    Thirty minutes to an hour covers most washer and cartridge jobs. Stuck cartridges or seized stems can take longer, especially on older faucets where scale has locked the parts in place and the valve body has to be soaked before anything comes loose. We plan for it.
