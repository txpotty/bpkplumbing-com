    Condensing Tankless Repair in West Jordan, UT

    Condensing tankless heaters pull extra heat out of the exhaust and make acidic condensate that has to drain somewhere. That condensate runs through a trap and, where code or the manual calls for it, a neutralizer filled with limestone or marble chips before it reaches the drain. Clogged traps shut the unit down. We clean the trap, refill the neutralizer and check the PVC vent for sag or the wrong slope.

    Igniter & Flame Rod Repair in West Jordan, UT

    Gas tankless heaters light with a hot surface or spark igniter, then prove flame with a flame rod. An oxidized flame rod gives a weak microamp reading and the board shuts the gas valve after a few seconds. We clean or replace the rod, look for a cracked igniter or a lazy spark, and confirm the gas valve opens on call. Five-minute fix sometimes.

    Navien Condensate and Fan Repair in West Jordan

    Navien's E016 is a heat exchanger overheat, and scale or a backed-up condensate line is usually behind it. We also read fan speed from the service menu and compare it to spec, since a fan that cannot reach target RPM will cause flame and exhaust faults on the same unit. Clean trap, clean screen, retest.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Repair in West Jordan

    Minor fixes like a flame rod or a flow sensor usually run $150 to $350. A heat exchanger or control board out of warranty can push a repair past $800, and at that point we price a replacement beside it. You see the number first.

    Hard water drops scale inside the heat exchanger every time it heats, and those passages are small. Skip the yearly flush and the unit runs hotter, loses output at high flow and eventually locks out on an overheat or scale code, and that can turn a routine descale into an exchanger replacement. The flush takes about an hour.

    Codes fire when a sensor reads something outside its range: a blocked vent, a weak flame signal, low flow or exhaust running too hot. Every code has its own test.

    Most take one to three hours on site. Descaling alone is closer to an hour and a half, while a part that has to be ordered, like a control board, usually means a second visit within a few business days. You will know before the tech leaves.

    Low flow at a single fixture, a dirty inlet filter, a failing flow sensor or scale will all cause temperature swings. A crossed hot and cold line at a single-handle faucet can do it too, which is a fixture problem, not a heater problem.

    Temperature swings, hot water that cuts out partway through a shower and any code on the display are the usual ones. A rotten egg smell near a gas unit means leave the house and call the gas company.

    With yearly descaling, 15 to 20 years is common on gas tankless heaters. Skip maintenance on hard water and trouble can start in three to five years.

    Cleaning the inlet filter and wiping down the intake screen are fair homeowner jobs. The sealed burner compartment is off limits, because a misaligned gasket or loose gas fitting there can leak exhaust or gas into the house, and many warranties are voided by unlicensed work.
