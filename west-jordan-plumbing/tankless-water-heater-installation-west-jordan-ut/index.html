    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Installation in West Jordan

    The cost for tankless water heater installation in West Jordan can range from $3,000 to $6,000, based on the unit type (gas or electric), brand, capacity and complexity of the installation, such as gas line upgrades or new venting. This price typically includes the unit and professional labor. We provide a detailed, no-obligation estimate after an on-site assessment.

    While it may seem appealing, tankless water heater installation is complex and should only be performed by a licensed professional. It involves gas lines, high-voltage electricity, water connections and detailed venting systems, all of which require specialized knowledge and adherence to safety codes. Incorrect installation can be dangerous and cancel warranties.

    With annual descaling, tankless units often last 15 to 20 years. Skipping maintenance in hard water areas can shorten that significantly.

    We install Rinnai, Navien, Noritz and Rheem tankless water heaters. Each brand offers different features, efficiency levels and warranties.

    Sizing a tankless water heater depends on your household's peak hot water demand, measured in gallons per minute (GPM), and the temperature rise needed. Our plumbers will count the hot water fixtures in your home and review your usage patterns to recommend the appropriate GPM and BTU rating for your needs in West Jordan.

    In Utah, plumbing and often gas or electrical permits are usually required for tankless water heater installation. BPK Plumbing handles all necessary permit applications and makes sure your installation passes all required inspections by local authorities.

    To maintain efficiency and extend the lifespan of your tankless unit, annual flushing to remove mineral buildup is recommended, especially in areas with hard water. Checking and cleaning the air filter (for gas models) and inspecting connections are also part of regular maintenance. BPK Plumbing offers comprehensive maintenance services.

    Signs you might need a new water heater, tankless or otherwise, include a lack of hot water, rust-colored water, strange noises from the unit or frequent leaks. For tankless systems specifically, frequent error codes or a significant drop in hot water flow could point to a need for service or replacement.
