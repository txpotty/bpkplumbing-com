    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Replacement in West Jordan

    The cost of toilet replacement in West Jordan, UT usually ranges from $400 to $800, including the new toilet fixture and labor. This can vary based on the toilet model you choose, any necessary repairs to the flange or subfloor, and the difficulty of the installation.

    Frequent clogs, cracks in the porcelain, constant repairs, rocking that returns after resetting and very high water use are common signs. Toilets more than 25 years old are often good candidates for replacement.

    The 'rough-in' measurement is the distance from the finished wall behind the toilet to the center of the drainpipe. Common rough-ins are 10, 12, or 14 inches. It is crucial for selecting a new toilet that fits correctly without extensive changes to your bathroom plumbing.

    It is possible for handy homeowners, but old toilets are heavy and flange problems are common once the toilet is pulled. A pro replacement avoids cracked porcelain, leaks and damaged floors.

    Mineral deposits reduce the life of tankless and tank water heaters alike, so regular flushing or descaling is worth the time. Hard water can lead to mineral buildup in toilet tanks and bowls, affecting flush mechanisms and causing stains. When replacing a toilet, we can recommend models with surfaces designed to resist mineral buildup and discuss water softening options.

    Many replacements take 1.5 to 2.5 hours. If the flange or subfloor needs repair, it can take longer.

    Signs of failing internal toilet parts include a toilet that runs constantly, a weak or incomplete flush, a handle that needs jiggling, or visible wear and tear on components like the flapper, fill valve, or flush valve. While some parts can be replaced individually, an older toilet with multiple failing components might benefit more from a full replacement.

    Key signs include persistent leaks that are hard to pinpoint or fix, a cracked toilet bowl or tank, frequent clogs despite proper use, excessive rocking or instability, or a toilet that is very old and inefficient, using too much water per flush. If repair costs approach the price of a new toilet, replacement is usually the better choice.
