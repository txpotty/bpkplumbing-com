    While both sump pumps and sewage ejector pumps remove water, their applications are different. BPK Plumbing provides expert guidance and installation for both in West Jordan. A sewage ejector pump, however, is designed for wastewater containing solids from toilets, sinks and showers in below-grade plumbing.     Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in West Jordan

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin typically costs $900 to $2,000. A new ejector system with basin, venting and floor work usually ranges from $2,500 to $6,000 or more.

    Regular maintenance for a sewage ejector pump involves periodically checking the basin for debris, confirming the check valve works correctly and testing the float switch. It is also wise to avoid flushing non-biodegradable items. BPK Plumbing offers maintenance services to keep your pump running efficiently and prevent surprise breakdowns.

    Installing a sewage ejector pump is a complex job that involves plumbing, electrical and sometimes excavation work. It requires specialized knowledge to get sizing, venting and code compliance right. We firmly recommend hiring a licensed professional like BPK Plumbing to avoid costly errors, safety hazards and potential code violations.

    Pump replacements typically take two to four hours. New systems with floor excavation and venting typically take one to two days.

    We recommend having your sewage ejector pump inspected annually, especially if it is in a high-use area. Regular inspections can identify potential issues early, extend the life of your pump and prevent unexpected failures and costly backups. This is particularly important for older units.

    Signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include unusual noises from the pump or pit, frequent cycling on and off, slow drainage from basement fixtures or a sewage odor coming from the area. If you notice any of these issues, it is best to have a professional inspect your system to prevent a complete failure and costly backups.

    A sewage ejector pump is designed to handle solid waste and wastewater from plumbing fixtures below the main sewer line, like toilets and sinks, discharging it into the sewer system. A sump pump, in contrast, removes groundwater from a basement or crawl space, only handling clear water to prevent flooding. They serve different purposes for different types of water.

    A standard sewage ejector pump installation usually takes one to two days. This includes excavating the pit, setting the basin and pump, connecting the plumbing and completing the electrical hookups. More complex installations, like those requiring significant trenching or electrical panel upgrades, may take longer.
