    A slab leak is a break in a water line running under a concrete foundation. Basements are common in Utah homes, which places water heaters, softeners and main drains below grade where slow leaks can go unnoticed. The usual signs are a warm patch of floor, a water bill that jumps or water running when nothing is on. Do not wait on it.

    Once a year is a good rule. Hard water speeds up sediment buildup, so homes with scale may need a flush more often, and a yearly check lets us test the relief valve, look at the anode rod and catch a weeping fitting before it rusts through the jacket. An anode rod often wears out in three to five years.

    Gurgling is the early one. After that come slow drains, a sewer smell and water rising in the shower pan, and when more than one fixture acts up at the same time, the clog is usually in the main line rather than a single trap. Main line clogs rarely clear on their own.

    Soft water and normal pressure prevent a lot of what we see. Grease out of the drains and a yearly pressure test help too, since anything over 80 psi wears out supply hoses and fixtures faster than people expect. A yearly inspection catches the rest.

    Watch for drips that will not stop, slow drains, banging or whistling pipes, a sudden drop in pressure, discolored water and wet spots with no obvious source. Any one of these points to a problem behind the wall or under the floor. Do not ignore them.
