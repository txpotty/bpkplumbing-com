    Bladder Pressure Tank Replacement in Millcreek

    Bladder tanks hold water inside a flexible butyl bag surrounded by pressurized air. When the bag ruptures, water fills the whole tank, there is no air cushion and the pump short cycles. A quick test is pressing the air valve on top, since water spitting out means the bladder has failed. We replace the tank, set the new pre-charge and verify the pump runs a full cycle. Most bladder tank swaps take about two to three hours.

    Pressure Tank Sizing and Upsizing in Millcreek

    Many older systems have a tank that is too small for the pump, so the motor starts every time someone washes their hands. Tank size is based on drawdown, which is the available water stored between cut-out and cut-in pressure. For example, a 10 gallon per minute pump usually needs at least 10 gallons of drawdown for a one minute run time. That often means an 80 to 119 gallon tank rather than the 20 gallon unit that came with the home. Upsizing is one of the best ways to extend pump life.

    Bladder-Style Pressure Tank Replacement in Millcreek, UT

    Most modern well pressure tanks are bladder-style, separating air and water for optimal efficiency. When the bladder fails, you'll experience symptoms like short-cycling of the well pump. BPK Plumbing carefully replaces these bladder-style tanks across Millcreek, UT, ensuring proper pre-charge pressure and system integration for long-lasting performance.

    Tank Tee, Gauge and Relief Valve Replacement in Millcreek

    The tank tee is the manifold that connects the tank, switch, gauge, relief valve and drain. Galvanized tees corrode and choke flow, especially with sandy or mineral-heavy water. We replace them with brass or stainless steel tank tees that hold up much longer. A new liquid-filled gauge gives reliable readings, and a fresh relief valve protects against a stuck switch. These small parts make a big difference in how well the system holds up.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Millcreek, UT

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop, typically at 30/50 or 40/60 psi. Pitted contacts, insect nests and a clogged sensing nipple are frequent causes of failure in well houses. We replace worn switches with Square D or similar models and clean or replace the nipple below it. A low-pressure cutoff switch can also be added to stop the pump if the well runs dry. We set the switch to match your new tank's pre-charge.
