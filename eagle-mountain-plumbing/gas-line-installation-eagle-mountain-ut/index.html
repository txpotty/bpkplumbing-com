    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Installation in Eagle Mountain

    Most gas line jobs in Eagle Mountain run $500 to $2,500. A single appliance hookup sits at the low end, while long runs, multiple connections or a buried line to the backyard push the price up, along with the choice between black iron and CSST. You get a written price first.

    Black iron and CSST cover most of our work. Rigid black iron is the pick for exposed runs, while CSST bends through finished walls with fewer joints and installs faster, so the choice usually comes down to where the pipe will run and how much wall you want opened. Both meet code.

    Yes, we install the gas line for a switch from electric to gas, like a water heater or dryer. That usually means extending an existing line or running a new one, plus venting for appliances that need it, and we check that every connection meets the appliance specs. Venting is part of the plan.

    Bonding ties the CSST to the home's electrical grounding system to reduce lightning damage. It is required by code and the manufacturer, so every install gets it.

    Gastite and OmegaFlex TracPipe are our usual CSST brands. Black iron pipe and fittings come from established suppliers, and every product we use is listed for gas and meets or exceeds local code requirements for the space it goes into. We use listed parts only.

    Sulfur smell, a hiss near a line and dead grass over a buried pipe are the warning signs. If you smell gas, leave and call the utility right away.

    Common materials are black iron and CSST, with copper tubing used in a few places for small lines where local code allows it. We choose the material based on the application, along with local code. Underground runs get polyethylene.

    Good gas lines rarely need scheduled inspections. A check during routine plumbing service is plenty, or right away if you notice an odd smell, a hissing sound or a spike in the gas bill. Homes from the 1980s and 1990s sometimes have gray polybutylene piping, which is prone to failure and a common reason to repipe. Older homes benefit from a look every 5 to 10 years.
