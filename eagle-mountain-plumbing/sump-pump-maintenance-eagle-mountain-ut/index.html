    Crawlspace and Window Well Pumps in Eagle Mountain, UT

    Not every sump sits in a basement. Crawlspaces and deep window wells often have small pumps that clear runoff after a storm.     Battery Backup Sump Pump Maintenance in Eagle Mountain, UT

    Backup pumps sit in the pit next to the main pump and run off a 12-volt battery when the power drops or the primary fails. Our plumber load tests the battery instead of just reading its voltage. Then we unplug the main pump and let the backup empty the pit on its own. Alarms get tested too.

    Home Sump Pump Maintenance in Eagle Mountain

    A typical house pit is about 18 inches across and two feet deep, and most of what goes wrong happens inside it. Our plumber lifts the pump out, cleans the screen and empties the sediment from the bottom. Then we run a full water test. Soils in Utah vary from clay that swells when wet to rocky benches and collapsible soils that settle when they take on water.

    Annual Sump Pump Check-Ups in Eagle Mountain

    Once a year is the minimum. We check the motor, impeller, float and discharge line on every annual visit. After that, we fill the basin and time the drawdown, since a slow pump-out is the first hint of a tired impeller or a restricted line.

    Float Switch Repair and Adjustment in Eagle Mountain, UT

    Float trouble causes more no-start calls than anything else. A tethered float can tangle on the pump body or catch on the side of the basin. Vertical floats stick when mineral crust builds up on the rod. For narrow basins where a float keeps catching, an electronic switch with no moving float is a good swap that ends the problem.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Check-Ups in Eagle Mountain, UT

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, so the wear shows up in the shaft, the bearings and the float rod. We check the rod for bends and the stops for slipping. Bearings get a listen. Rattling or a high whine at startup usually means the motor is near the end.

    Sump Pit Cleaning and Debris Removal in Eagle Mountain

    Silt and gravel wash into a pit through the drain tile every time it rains, and over a few years that layer can bury the intake screen. Our plumber drains the basin and digs the sediment out by hand. With the pit clean, the float swings without catching. The pump runs cooler too.
