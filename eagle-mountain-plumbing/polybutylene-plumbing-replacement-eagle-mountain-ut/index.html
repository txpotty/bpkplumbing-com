    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Eagle Mountain

    PEX replacements usually run $4,000 to $10,000, and copper often runs $8,000 to $15,000 or more. Attic access matters a lot. House size, fixture count and how easy it is to reach the attic or crawlspace are the main factors, and we give you a detailed written quote after looking at the house in person.

    Utah water is among the most mineral-heavy in the country in many areas, and scale builds up quickly in water heaters, faucets and appliances. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. Scale builds inside. Mineral scale cuts flow and adds strain to brittle pipe, which is why a softener loop during the repipe can make sense in hard water.

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water break down the plastic from the inside, making it brittle. Fittings and hot water lines often fail first.

    Gray, blue or black plastic pipe marked PB2110 at the water heater or under sinks is the clearest sign. The build year matters. A house from roughly 1978 to 1995 with this pipe and a past fitting leak should be replaced before the next failure.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper can last longer. In water that does not attack it, Type L copper can last 50 years or more, and both are major upgrades over poly.

    Most of our PEX replacements use Uponor AquaPEX with ProPEX expansion fittings. Viega PEX is another option. For copper, we use domestic Type L pipe, and we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    Our poly replacements carry a written warranty on materials and labor. Details are written up front. PEX-A jobs done by certified installers also get the maker's long-term warranty, which we register on your behalf.
