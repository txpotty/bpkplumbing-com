    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in South Jordan

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Swapping an identical plug-in pump is doable for a handy homeowner. Cutting a pit, wiring a new outlet and routing discharge are not, because a mistake there leads to a cracked slab, a shock hazard, or water pumped right back against the foundation in South Jordan. Those parts call for a licensed plumber.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Rust on the housing and constant on-off cycling are others, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. Past ten years, replace it.

    Our installs carry a written warranty on both parts and labor. You will see the exact length in your service agreement, and the pump manufacturer adds its own coverage, which on a quality cast iron pump usually runs three to five years from the date of purchase. We handle the registration.

    We strongly recommend professional installation. You are dealing with wiring, pipe and concrete in one job, and a bad seal on the lid, an undersized pit or a discharge that drains toward the house can leave you worse off than having no pump at all. Errors appear at the worst moment.

    Once a year is enough for most pumps, ideally before the wet season. A tune-up covers the float, the pit, the check valve and the discharge, and we confirm the discharge outlet outside is open, since a clogged outlet makes the pump run hard against a blocked line. The visit is short.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. The real answer depends on inflow and lift, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Bigger is not always better.

    Liberty Pumps, Zoeller and Wayne are our regulars. All three use cast iron housings and reliable switches, and all three have solid parts support through local supply houses, which matters more than a fancy feature list when something breaks. The pit size picks the model.
