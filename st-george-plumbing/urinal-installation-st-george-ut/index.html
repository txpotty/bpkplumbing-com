    New urinals and restroom changes usually require a plumbing permit. Simple like-for-like replacements usually do not, and we will confirm with your local building department.

    Constant running, cracked porcelain, persistent odor, leaks at the wall and slow drains all point to a fixture or valve that needs work.

    A washdown urinal uses a simple flushing action to rinse the bowl, relying on gravity and the slope of the drain. A siphonic urinal creates a siphonic action during flushing, which pulls waste more forcefully down the drain, offering more effective waste removal and usually better odor control.

    Flush urinals use a water flush to remove waste, while waterless urinals use a specialized trap system with a sealant liquid to prevent odors without water. Waterless urinals offer significant water savings but require specific maintenance for their sealant cartridges. Both types offer effective waste removal.

    Regular cleaning with appropriate cleaners is essential for both flush and waterless urinals. Waterless urinals also require periodic replacement of their sealant cartridges, typically every 1 to 3 months depending on usage. Proper maintenance prevents odors and clogs and keeps the fixture hygienic.
