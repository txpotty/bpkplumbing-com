    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in St. George

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet typically runs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout usually runs $200 to $500. With hydro jetting added, expect roughly $450 to $1,000, depending on how long the line is and how heavy the grease is. Nights and weekends run higher.

    We run RIDGID, Spartan and Gorlitz augers, plus sewer cameras and jetters from the same tier of commercial makers. They survive heavy daily use. Brand matters less than setup. Even a top machine can hurt a pipe with the wrong head, so picking the cutter gets as much attention as the machine.

    No permit is required for routine rooting. A new cleanout or a pipe repair may need one. Permits for repair work are on us.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. A root-choked main or a long jetting job may take 3 to 6 hours. We tell you up front. After looking at the cleanout and hearing which drains are slow, the tech can narrow the estimate.

    Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go years without it. A camera helps set the schedule. How quickly roots return after the last cut tells us if yearly or every-other-year service fits your line.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in St. George's established homes take the most damage. They also make our job riskier. Let us know if you used a chemical, because that water splashes when the trap comes off.

    Keep grease, coffee grounds and wipes out of drains. Hair strainers in tubs and showers help. Run hot water after using the disposal, and scrape plates into the trash before rinsing so less food goes down the kitchen line. Root-prone lines need cutting every year or two.

    Sewer pipes leak a little water vapor at the joints. Roots follow that moisture to the gap and push in. Once in, they grow quickly. In St. George, clay pipe with a joint every 2 to 3 feet and older cast iron with oakum joints are the most common targets.
