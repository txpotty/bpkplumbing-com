    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in St. George

    A standard sump pump replacement usually costs $500 to $1,200 installed. Battery backup systems usually add $800 to $1,800. Sewage ejector pumps generally cost $1,200 to $2,500.

    Most sump pumps last 7 to 10 years. Pumps that sit idle for long periods can fail sooner due to stuck switches.

    A battery backup sump pump system is a secondary pump powered by a battery, designed to turn on automatically if your primary sump pump loses power during an outage or fails. This is important for maintaining flood protection during power outages caused by severe weather.

    Most standard sump pump replacements can be finished within 2 to 4 hours. More complex installations, such as those requiring new pit excavation or extensive discharge line work, might take longer, up to a full day.

    While it may seem straightforward, professional sump pump replacement is strongly recommended. It involves electrical connections, proper plumbing for the discharge line, and making sure the pump is correctly sized and installed to prevent future flooding and comply with local St. George codes.

    To maintain your new sump pump, regularly check the pit for debris, test the pump monthly by pouring water into the pit, and make sure the discharge pipe is clear. An annual professional inspection by BPK Plumbing can also help lengthen its lifespan.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, powered by your home's electricity. A backup sump pump, usually battery-powered or water-powered, is a secondary system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or cannot keep up with heavy water inflow. Many St. George homes are better off with a backup system due to storm risks.

    If storms knock out power in your area or you have finished living space below grade, a backup is a smart investment. A battery unit with a high-water alarm typically runs six to ten hours of intermittent pumping on a full charge.
