    Pre-Purchase Plumbing Inspections in West Valley City

    A house can pass a general inspection and still have a sewer full of roots.     Gas Connection Safety Check in West Valley City, UT

    During a full inspection, we look at the visible gas piping and the connectors to each appliance. We check for old uncoated brass connectors, missing shutoffs, missing sediment traps and corrosion, and we test joints with an electronic detector. Any leak is reported on the spot. Older homes are where we find the most issues, usually at appliance connectors that have been there since the house was built.

    Preventive Maintenance Checks in West Valley City, UT

    A maintenance check is a lighter version of a full inspection for owners who want to stay ahead of problems. The visit covers flushing the heater, working the shutoffs, reading pressure and checking for slow drains and small leaks. It takes about an hour. Doing this every year or two adds years to a water heater and keeps the shutoffs working for the day you really need them.

    Pipe Material and Water Quality Review in West Valley City, UT

    Pipe material says a lot about what to expect from a house. Our plumber identifies the supply pipe, whether copper, CPVC, PEX, galvanized, poly or Kitec, and explains how each usually fails. Some need replacing soon. Polybutylene and Kitec are known for sudden failures, and galvanized slowly closes up with rust, so those get flagged with replacement options.

    Water Heater Inspections in West Valley City

    Water heaters give clues long before they fail. We read the serial number for age, look for rust at the fittings and the base, check the venting and test the T and P relief valve. We check tankless units for scale. On gas units, we look at the burner and flame, check for a sediment trap and confirm the vent is drafting so combustion gases go outside.

    Hard Water Damage Assessment in West Valley City

    Mineral deposits reduce the life of tankless and tank water heaters alike, so regular flushing or descaling is worth the time. Hard water leaves scale in pipes, fixtures and water heaters. We look for crusty aerators, scaled showerheads, slow-filling toilets and sediment in the water heater, and we can run a simple hardness test on the spot. Then we explain your options. Softeners, filters and scale reducers all help, and we suggest the one that matches the reading we took.
