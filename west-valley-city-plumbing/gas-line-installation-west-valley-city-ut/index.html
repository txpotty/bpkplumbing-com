    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Installation in West Valley City

    A simple indoor line for a range or dryer usually costs $350 to $1,000. Expect $1,000 to $3,500 for outdoor runs to a grill, fire pit or pool heater, depending on distance, trenching and line size.

    It is a dangerous job best left to licensed professionals. Improper installation can cause leaks, explosions or carbon monoxide poisoning, and most jurisdictions require a licensed plumber to do the work and pull the permit. The law usually settles it.

    We strongly advise against it. A badly joined or undersized line can leak or starve the appliances, and most jurisdictions require licensed work and an inspection.

    Black iron and CSST cover most of our work. Black iron is rigid and suits exposed runs, while CSST bends through finished walls with fewer joints and installs faster, so the choice usually comes down to where the pipe will run and how much wall you want opened. Both meet code.

    A rotten egg smell is the main warning sign of a leak. A hissing sound near a pipe or a patch of dead grass over a buried line are others, and if you notice any of them, leave the building, call your gas provider from outside, and then call us. Do not try to find it yourself.

    Bonding ties the CSST to the home's electrical grounding system to reduce lightning damage. It is required by code and the manufacturer, so every install gets it.

    Common materials are black iron and CSST, with copper tubing used in a few places for small lines where local code allows it. Our team picks the material by application, along with local code. Underground runs get polyethylene.

    A single appliance hookup takes a few hours to a day. Bigger jobs with outdoor runs can take one to two days, and permits and inspections add a few days on top of the work itself, depending on how fast the city schedules. We book the inspection early.
