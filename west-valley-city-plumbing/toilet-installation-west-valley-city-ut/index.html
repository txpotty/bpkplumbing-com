    Comfort Height Toilet Installation in West Valley City, UT

    Comfort height bowls stand about two inches taller than standard ones. Think about who will use it before you buy. Shorter adults and kids sometimes find the taller seat leaves their feet dangling, while anyone with bad knees or a hip replacement usually finds it much easier to sit and stand. Installation is the same as a standard bowl.

    Rough-In Toilet Installation in West Valley City, UT

    Rough-in is the plumbing that goes in before walls and floors close up. On a toilet, that is a vented 3 inch drain and a cold water stub. We set the drain 12 inches from the framed wall plus the thickness of drywall, so the finished rough-in lands right where the fixture expects it. Then the inspector sees it open.

    ADA-Compliant Toilet Installation in West Valley City

    ADA toilets put the seat 17 to 19 inches off the floor, and the centerline sits 16 to 18 inches from the side wall. That spacing leaves room for a grab bar. We measure the existing drain before ordering, since moving a drain two inches to hit the ADA centerline means opening the floor. Elongated bowls are standard here.

    Two-Piece Toilet Installation in West Valley City

    Two-piece toilets come with the tank and bowl separate, joined by a rubber gasket and two or three bolts. The tank goes on before the bowl is set. Even tightening, alternating sides, matters more than force, since uneven pressure either cracks the china or leaves one bolt weeping. It should not wobble.

    Low-Flow WaterSense Toilet Installation in West Valley City, UT

    WaterSense toilets are capped at 1.28 gallons per flush, and some dual-flush models drop to around 0.8 for liquid waste. That adds up fast. Replacing a single 3.5 gallon toilet in a household of four can save several thousand gallons a year, and some water utilities have offered rebates on high-efficiency models, so it is worth asking yours. Pick a strong MaP rating.

    Smart Toilet Installation in West Valley City, UT

    Smart toilets with heated seats and built-in bidets need two things the old toilet did not: power and a dedicated water connection. Most need a GFCI outlet close enough for the cord. The water connection and mounting are ours, and when an outlet has to be added we schedule a licensed electrician instead of doing that wiring. Then we test every button.

    Commercial Toilet Installation in West Valley City, UT

    Commercial restrooms take a beating, so we install vitreous china bowls with flushometer valves rather than tank toilets. Flushometers want a 1 inch supply line and steady pressure. Before setting fixtures in an office or shop, we check that the building supply can deliver the flow the valve is rated for, often 20 to 25 gallons per minute at the fixture. Fixture counts follow code.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in West Valley City

    Setting a customer-supplied standard toilet usually costs $200 to $400 in labor. With a mid-range toilet included, most installs land between $450 and $900. Wall-hung systems with an in-wall carrier run $1,500 to $3,500 or more, mostly because the wall has to be opened, framed for the carrier and finished again afterward. We quote a flat price.

    TOTO and American Standard do well in independent flush tests, and Kohler is close behind, with parts at most supply houses. For a budget, Gerber and Mansfield hold up. We match the model to your rough-in and how the bathroom gets used.

    Figure one to two hours for a standard floor-mount toilet. Bigger jobs take a day or more. Wall-hung carriers and slab relocations open walls or floors, so they get booked as separate projects with cure time built in.

    It depends on the floor and the flange. On a sound flange, a same-spot swap is fair game for a handy owner. Hire out new tile jobs, broken flanges, wall-hung carriers and relocations, since mistakes there hide under the floor.

    Homeowners set basic toilets all the time. Flange height and bolt torque are where it goes wrong. A flange that sits below the finished floor needs an extender or a thicker seal, and a nut tightened one turn too far can crack the base, so on new floors and wall-hung toilets a pro install is the safer bet.

    A one-piece toilet is cast as a single unit, so there is no tank-to-bowl joint to leak. Expect a higher price and more weight. Two-piece toilets join tank and bowl with a gasket, so they are easier to carry upstairs and you can replace one half if it cracks. Both flush well.

    Adding or moving a toilet almost always needs one. Swapping in the same place usually does not. An inspector checks the new drain and vent while things are still open, and we book that visit around your remodel schedule.

    Plan on one to three hours for a straight swap. Most of that is prep, not setting the bowl. Jobs that need a new flange or a patch of rotted subfloor can stretch toward half a day, and we will tell you on arrival if yours is one of them.
