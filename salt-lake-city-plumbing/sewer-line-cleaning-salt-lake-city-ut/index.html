    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Line Cleaning in Salt Lake City

    The cost of sewer line cleaning in Salt Lake City, UT typically ranges from $200 to $600, depending on the severity and location of the clog, and the method required, such as snaking or hydro jetting. More complex issues like extensive tree root removal or deep blockages can sometimes cost more. We provide upfront, clear pricing after a diagnostic assessment.

    Drain cleaning typically addresses blockages within individual drain pipes connected to sinks, showers, or toilets. Sewer line cleaning, on the other hand, deals with the main line that carries all wastewater from your home to the municipal sewer system or septic tank. A sewer line issue affects multiple fixtures.

    In most cases, sewer line cleaning methods like snaking and hydro jetting are non-invasive and do not require digging. We access the line through existing cleanouts. Digging is usually only necessary if there is a severe structural issue like a collapsed pipe requiring replacement, which we would confirm with a camera inspection first.

    For most homes in Salt Lake City, annual or twice-yearly sewer line cleaning is enough, especially if you have mature trees nearby or an older plumbing system. If you have frequent clogs or a high-usage commercial property, more frequent cleaning might be wise to prevent major backups.

    We firmly advise against chemical drain cleaners for sewer line clogs. These products often contain harsh chemicals that can corrode pipes, especially older ones, and are usually ineffective against severe blockages like tree roots. They can also pose a health risk and make professional cleaning more difficult.

    We use industry-leading equipment from trusted brands such as Spartan Tool, RIDGID, and Gorlitz for our sewer cameras, drain snakes, and hydro jetting machines. These brands are known for their durability and effectiveness in tackling tough sewer line blockages.

    During our diagnostic camera inspection, if we discover that your sewer line is broken, collapsed, or severely damaged, we will tell you immediately. In such cases, cleaning might only be a temporary fix, and we would discuss options for sewer line repair or replacement, laying out all available solutions and costs.

    Older homes often have clay or cast iron, and some have Orangeburg fiber pipe. Most homes built in recent decades have ABS or PVC. Homes from the 1980s and 1990s sometimes have polybutylene piping, which is prone to failure and a common reason to repipe.
