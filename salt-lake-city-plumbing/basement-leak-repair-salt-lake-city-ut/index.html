    How We Fix Basement Leaks in Salt Lake City, UT

    Groundwater Pressure Relief in Salt Lake City

    High groundwater pushes up through the slab as well as in through the walls. Water rising through floor cracks or around a floor drain during wet weather is the usual sign. Under-slab drain pipe tied to a sump lowers the water level beneath the floor. It is not a quick job.

    Waterproofing for Older Utah Homes in Salt Lake City, UT

    Older basements were often built with block walls and no exterior membrane. Older homes in Salt Lake City may still have galvanized steel supply lines that corrode inside and cause low pressure and rusty water. Water collects inside hollow block cores, and weep holes at the base let it drain into a perimeter channel. Clay tile drains fail too.

    Clogged Drain Tile Repair in Salt Lake City

    Footing drains clog with silt and roots over the years, and once they stop flowing the water table at the wall rises. A camera run from the sump inlet shows us where the tile is plugged. Jetting clears it. If the old clay tile has collapsed, it gets replaced with new perforated pipe.

    Supply Line Leak Repair in Salt Lake City

    Water lines along a basement ceiling can leak at fittings, valves or pinholes. Water softeners are a common fixture in Utah homes, and many newer houses are built with a softener loop already in place. We cut out the bad section and replace it with copper or PEX, and if we find more than two or three pinholes on one run, we recommend repiping that run instead of patching it again. Repiping a basement ceiling is usually a one-day job.

    Water Heater and Appliance Leak Repair in Salt Lake City, UT

    Water heaters, softeners and washing machines often sit in basements, and their leaks pool on the floor out of sight. A tank leaking at the seam is done. Valve and connector leaks are usually a same-visit fix, and an automatic shutoff sensor is worth adding.

    Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Salt Lake City

    A sump pump lives in a pit cut below floor level and kicks on when a float rises. Most failures come from a stuck float switch, a burned-out motor, or a check valve that lets the discharge water fall back into the pit and short-cycles the pump. We test yours with a bucket. Add a battery backup if storms knock out power.

    Exterior Waterproofing Solutions in Salt Lake City, UT

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing, cleaning the wall, and applying a membrane with a drainage board over it. It is the most thorough fix and the most disruptive one. Expect a few days of work. We bring in a waterproofing partner for the membrane and handle the footing drain and its tie-in to a sump or daylight outlet ourselves.

    Wall Crack Sealing for Basements in Salt Lake City

    Injection works on poured concrete walls, not block. Ports go along the crack, the face gets sealed, and resin goes in from the bottom up until it fills the full depth. Epoxy adds strength. Polyurethane is better for active leaks because it foams on contact with water.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Leak Repair in Salt Lake City

    Most wet basement repairs fall between $500 and $5,000, and the spread comes from what is causing the water. Downspout and grading fixes cost the least. Interior drains cost more since the slab gets cut, and exterior work costs most because of the digging. You get the price in writing before work starts.

    For sump pumps we mostly install Zoeller and Liberty, with Wayne for some battery backups. Cast iron housings outlast plastic ones. For crack injection we use two-part polyurethane or epoxy kits from established commercial suppliers, and for interior drains we use perforated pipe with a dimpled wall membrane from the same manufacturer so the pieces fit together.

    Simple pipe repairs and crack injection usually do not need a permit. Larger work is a different story. Stack replacement, new sumps and backwater valves often need one, and we handle it.

    Shut off the main water valve first, then call us. Freezing temperatures are a regular part of Utah winters, and pipes in garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls can freeze during cold stretches. We cut out the split section, replace it with copper or PEX, and pressure test the line before turning the water back on to the rest of the house. Insulating that run comes next.

    Tightening a loose fitting or replacing a washer hose is a reasonable DIY task. Cast iron stack cracks are another matter. Slab leaks and sump wiring need a pro, since a mistake can flood the floor or create a shock risk.

    Gutters and downspouts do most of the work. Clear them and extend the outlets away from the walls. Inside, pour a bucket of water into the sump pit every spring to make sure the pump kicks on, check the water heater and washer hoses each year, and replace rubber washer hoses with braided steel ones every five years or so.

    It depends on the scope. Small pipe repairs usually skip the permit. Bigger work like a new stack or a backwater valve may need one, and we pull it.

    Look for damp spots on walls or floors, a musty smell, and white chalky deposits on concrete. Those deposits are efflorescence, left behind when water evaporates. Peeling paint low on the wall, rust on the water heater base, and a water bill that keeps creeping up with no change in use all point toward a leak worth testing. Smell often comes first.
