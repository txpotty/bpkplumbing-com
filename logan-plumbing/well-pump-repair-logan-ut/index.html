    Pressure Switch Replacement in Logan

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing. After swapping the switch and clearing the tube, we set cut-in and cut-out to match the tank, usually 40 and 60 psi. This is among the least expensive well repairs.

    Well Pump Brands We Service in Logan, UT

    Goulds, Grundfos, Franklin Electric and Myers make most of the pumps we pull in this area, and BPK Plumbing services all of them. We work on old standard pumps and newer variable speed drives alike. Common parts are stocked. A pump with life left in it is worth repairing, and that saves the cost of a new motor and the labor to set it.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Logan, UT

    Electrical faults cause a big share of well calls, from a tripped breaker to a burned contact in the pressure switch. After that, we look at the capacitor and relay in the control box. We lock out the power before testing anything. All wiring and splices meet Utah electrical code before the pump restarts.

    Pressure Tank Problems in Logan

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. A waterlogged tank or a ruptured bladder causes most of it. Precharge gets checked on an empty tank. Set it 2 psi under cut-in, which means about 38 psi for a 40/60 switch.

    No-Water Same-Day Repair in Logan

    A dead pump leaves the house without toilets, showers or drinking water. BPK Plumbing answers emergency well pump calls throughout Logan and nearby areas ahead of scheduled jobs.     Low Water Pressure Diagnosis in Logan

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. A clogged sediment filter, a pressure switch set too low or a leak in the yard line can all cause it. Sometimes the pump is fine and the well is simply producing less water than the house is asking for during peak use. We test each one.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Logan, UT

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. That rapid cycling burns out pumps early. We check the air charge, inspect the bladder and recharge or replace the tank, then confirm the pump runs at least a minute per cycle. Steadier pressure follows.
