    Hydrojetting for Cast Iron Pipes in Logan, UT

    Older cast iron drains rust and scale from the inside, leaving a rough surface that grabs debris. A jet clears that crust. Thin or cracked cast iron gets gentler settings or a lining recommendation instead.

    Whole-House Drain Jetting in Logan

    Slow drains in several rooms usually mean buildup in a main or branch line. The main gets jetted from the cleanout, and branches from vents or fixtures. Most homes take two to three hours.

    Main Sewer Line Hydrojetting in Logan

    The main sewer carries every drain in the house, so a blockage there backs up the lowest fixture first. We jet the full main to the street tap. Four-inch mains get a heavy nozzle and high flow to push debris downstream.

    Sewer Camera Inspection Before Hydrojetting in Logan

    Jetting a broken pipe can make the break worse. We camera every line before we set up the jetter. The video shows the pipe material, the condition of each joint, and any cracks, offsets or sags, which tells us the nozzle and pressure to use or whether we should not jet that line at all.

    Hard Water Scale Removal in Logan, UT

    Scale builds slowly inside drain pipe, narrowing it a little more each year. Utah water is among the hardest in the country in many areas, and scale builds up quickly in water heaters, faucets and appliances. A jetter with a scouring nozzle strips that scale from the walls, and on heavily scaled cast iron we may follow up with a descaling chain head to finish the job. You can see the change on video.

    Grease Trap Line Cleaning in Logan, UT

    Restaurant kitchen lines carry hot grease that hardens as it cools. Much of it coats the pipe before it ever reaches the interceptor. We jet the line from the kitchen to the interceptor in short passes, which keeps loosened grease from plugging the pipe farther down, and we coordinate with your pump-out company. Quarterly works for most.

    Hydrojetting vs. Snaking in Logan, UT

    Snaking pokes a hole through a clog. Jetting cleans the whole pipe. For a single clog in an otherwise clean line, snaking is cheaper and works fine, but for a line that clogs every few months, jetting removes the buildup that keeps catching debris.
