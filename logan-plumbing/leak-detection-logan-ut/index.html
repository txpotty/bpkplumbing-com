    Pool Leak Detection in Logan

    A pool losing more than a quarter inch a day beyond evaporation probably has a leak. We start with a bucket test. Then we pressure test the suction and return lines, use an underwater hydrophone to listen, and dye test around the skimmer, lights and main drain to find the exact crack or fitting that is losing water.

    Gas Leak Detection in Logan

    A faint sulfur smell near the range or furnace needs a professional check. Get out first if it is strong. Our licensed plumbers use a combustible gas detector and soap solution on fittings, appliance connectors and the meter riser, then pressure test the line to find a leak too small to smell, with repairs made to the fuel gas code before the gas comes back on.

    PEX and Copper Leak Detection in Logan, UT

    On a ground microphone, copper leaks are easy to hear. PEX is quieter, since plastic pipe does not carry sound well, so we lean more on thermal scans, moisture readings and tracer gas when the house is plumbed in it. We handle both every week. Pinholes in copper and failed crimp fittings in PEX top the list of what we find.

    Hidden Wall and Ceiling Leaks in Logan

    Bubbling paint, soft drywall and a musty smell come up on most of our calls. All of them mean water. A thermal scan shows the wet patch, and a moisture meter confirms it. Acoustic tools help when the leak is on a pressurized line, and once the source is pinned down we open one small access hole rather than following the stain around the room.

    Fixture and Appliance Leaks in Logan, UT

    One running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons daily. Worn faucet cartridges, softener valves and water heater connectors add more. We dye test tanks, check flappers and fill valves, and inspect supply lines and water heater fittings, and most of these repairs take under an hour with parts from the truck. They are inexpensive repairs.

    Gas Line Leak Locating in Logan

    Gas leaks are found by test, not by nose. Our plumbers pressure test the system with a gauge, then check each joint with a detector and soap solution. We also walk the yard line, since a dead strip of grass over a buried gas pipe can signal a leak that has run for a long time. Utility coordination is included.

    Slab Leak Detection in Logan

    Concrete slab homes have supply lines buried in sand below the floor, and a pinhole there can run for months before anyone notices. Look for warm tile first. We pressure test the hot and cold sides separately, then listen through the slab with a ground microphone and confirm with a thermal camera on hot water lines.

    Slab Leak Signs in Logan, UT

    Warm floor spots, cracked tile and a meter that never stops are the common signs. So is the sound of water with everything off. We isolate hot and cold lines with pressure tests, then trace and listen through the slab, and once it is located we go over spot repair, reroute and repipe options with prices for each.
