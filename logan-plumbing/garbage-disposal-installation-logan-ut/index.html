    Standard Disposal Installation in Logan

    Most households in Logan do well with a continuous-feed unit between 1/3 and 1/2 horsepower. We remove the old unit if there is one, set a new flange in fresh plumber's putty, hang the disposal on a new mount and connect it to the trap with a new discharge tube and gasket. Wiring and testing come last. A smooth spin and a clean drain tell us it is right.

    High-Capacity Disposal Installation in Logan, UT

    Busy kitchens get the most from a 1 HP unit, like an InSinkErator Evolution or a Waste King Legend model. We check that the circuit can handle the motor and that the discharge path is sized for the heavier flow, since a big unit feeding a narrow or poorly sloped trap arm still clogs. Bigger units are heavier. We support them while mounting.

    Disposal Installation for Double Sinks in Logan, UT

    Double bowl sinks put the disposal on one side and a continuous waste or end outlet tee on the other. The layout keeps both bowls draining and stops the disposal from pushing water into the second bowl, a problem when the tee is too close to the discharge. Heavy-wall fittings last longer.

    Dishwasher Connection With Disposal Installation in Logan

    Every new disposal has a plug sealing the dishwasher inlet. We knock it out with a screwdriver and hammer, fish the plug out of the chamber and connect the hose with a new clamp. Forgetting the plug floods the dishwasher. An air gap or high loop goes in too, which keeps sink water from running back into the dishwasher.

    Batch-Feed Units for Kitchens Without a Switch in Logan

    Batch-feed disposals only run when the stopper lid is in place, which adds safety for families with kids. They need no wall switch, so they are a good choice for kitchens where adding a switch would mean cutting into finished drywall or tile backsplash. Mounting and drains are standard.

    Disposals in Remodels and New Builds in Logan

    On new builds and remodels in Logan, we work with your contractor so the disposal, the sink and the dishwasher are planned together. That usually means a dedicated circuit for the disposal, a drain stub set low enough for the trap and a dishwasher hose path that allows a proper high loop or air gap. Early planning prevents rework.
