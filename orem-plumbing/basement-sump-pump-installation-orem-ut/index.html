    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Orem

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. The backup is a common add.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Every two to three months, and before and after the wet season. Dump a bucket of water into the pit slowly, then watch the float lift, the pump start, the water drop, and the pump shut off cleanly without humming or rattling. That is the whole test.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Rust on the housing and constant on-off cycling are others, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. After about ten years, plan on replacing it.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. A sticking float or rust streaks on the housing also mean trouble, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Replace it before the next storm.

    Water heater replacements, gas line work and new bathroom plumbing usually need a permit and an inspection from the local building department. New pits, new circuits and new discharge routes are the parts most likely to need a plumbing permit. The local requirements get checked before we start, and when a permit applies we pull it and schedule the inspection ourselves. You do not deal with the office.

    We strongly recommend professional installation. The job combines electrical, plumbing and concrete work, and a bad seal on the lid, an undersized pit or a discharge that drains toward the house can leave you worse off than having no pump at all. Mistakes show up in storms.

    Labor on our sump pump installs is warrantied, typically for one year. The pump carries its own manufacturer warranty, usually one to five years depending on the brand and model, and we write the exact coverage on your invoice so you know which part to call about if something fails. Save the paperwork.
