    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer in Orem

    Annual residential testing runs $75 to $150 per assembly. Repairs with a rebuild kit typically run $150 to $450. Residential installs run about $300 to $1,200, while a commercial RPZ is often $1,500 to $5,000 installed with its drain and enclosure.

    Most water providers, including those around Orem, require a test annually. Some also require a test right after installation, after a repair, or any time the assembly is moved to a new spot. Check your notice for the deadline.

    Once a year is the usual requirement. Installation, repair and relocation can each trigger a test too. The deadline is on your notice, and we can usually get there within days.

    Testing must be done by a certified backflow tester using calibrated equipment. Reports from anyone else are rejected. The gauge also has to be calibrated within the past year, and that calibration date goes right on the report form.

    An RPZ has two checks with a relief valve between them, and it is rated for high-hazard lines. A DCVA has two checks and no relief valve, so it is used for lower hazards like many fire lines and some irrigation systems. Local rules pick the device.

    Water providers may send follow-up notices, charge fees or eventually suspend service. Scheduling early avoids these problems. We can often fit urgent tests in on short notice, even when the deadline is only a few days away.

    Installation and repair should be left to a licensed plumber who holds tester certification. Installing the wrong device, mounting a PVB too low or burying an RPZ in a pit can each fail inspection and leave the water unprotected. Fines can follow.

    Look for a steady leak from the device, a sudden drop in water pressure or discolored water at the tap. An RPZ that discharges from its relief port whenever pressure shifts usually has a leaking check. Get it tested soon.
