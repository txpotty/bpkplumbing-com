    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Provo

    Underground water line leaks are often hard to detect until significant damage has occurred. Common signs include sudden spikes in your water bill, damp spots in your yard, or a clear drop in water pressure. Our team uses acoustic leak detection and ground-penetrating radar to accurately pinpoint these hidden leaks in Provo, reducing the need for extensive excavation. We repair breaks caused by shifting soil, tree root intrusion, or aging pipes, restoring your water service quickly.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Provo

    If your service line runs under a driveway, pool deck or mature landscaping, trenchless replacement can save a lot of restoration cost. We dig small pits at each end and pull new HDPE or PEX pipe through using a pneumatic boring tool or pipe pulling equipment. The process usually takes one day. You keep your pavers and trees, and the new line carries a long manufacturer warranty.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in Provo, UT

    A full break can flood a yard, wash out soil under walkways and leave your home without water. Our emergency crews shut off the supply, locate the break and set up a temporary bypass when the repair will take more than a few hours. Most emergency breaks are repaired the same day.     Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Provo, UT

    Many homes built before the 1970s still have galvanized steel service lines that rust from the inside. Water softeners are a common fixture in Utah homes, and many newer houses are built with a softener loop already in place. Hard water speeds up scale buildup, which chokes flow and causes rust-colored water. Patching galvanized pipe seldom holds up because the surrounding pipe is just as corroded. We usually recommend replacing the full run from meter to house with copper or PEX, which restores pressure and ends repeat leaks.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Provo

    Whether your home has modern PEX piping or traditional copper lines, our technicians are skilled in repairing both. PEX offers flexibility and corrosion resistance, while copper is known for its durability. We carry a full stock of appropriate fittings and tools for each material, ensuring a proper, code-compliant repair. Our expertise extends to identifying common failure points for each type, such as fitting issues with PEX or pinhole leaks in older copper lines, common in homes with harder water.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Provo

    Many water line problems are made worse by high incoming pressure. A failed pressure reducing valve can push well over 80 psi into your plumbing, which stresses the service line and every fixture. We test static pressure with a gauge and replace worn regulators from brands like Watts and Zurn Wilkins. We also swap seized gate valves for full-port ball valves so you can shut the water off fast in an emergency.

    PEX Water Line Repair in Provo, UT

While PEX is less prone to leaks than older materials, damage can still occur from improper installation, rodent chewing, or impact. Our team is fully experienced in PEX repair, using specialized crimping tools and fittings to make strong, leak-proof connections. We make sure your PEX system continues to perform well.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Provo

    A spot repair on an accessible service line usually runs $350 to $1,500 depending on depth and access. Full replacement from meter to house usually costs $1,800 to $5,500, and trenchless replacement often falls between $3,000 and $7,000. We give you a written price before starting.

    Common signs of a broken water line include unusually high water bills, a sudden drop in water pressure, wet spots in your yard or on pavement that don't dry, discolored water, or the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use. For homes on slab foundations, look for cracks in the foundation or warm spots on the floor.

    Common signs include a wet or unusually green patch of yard, a hissing sound near the meter, low pressure at every fixture and a water bill that climbs without explanation. Dirty or rusty water can also point to a damaged line. If your meter moves with all fixtures off, you likely have a leak.

    Water lines can leak or break due to several factors, including aging pipes (corrosion in galvanized steel or copper), shifting soil, tree root intrusion, high water pressure, ground freezing and thawing cycles, and accidental damage during excavation. Poor initial installation can also contribute to early failure.

    In most communities the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. The homeowner is responsible for the service line from the meter to the house. We confirm which side the leak is on before quoting you.

    Preventing future water line issues involves a few key steps: being mindful of landscaping near buried lines, quickly fixing minor leaks, and considering a whole-home water pressure regulator if your pressure is consistently high. Regular plumbing inspections can also help identify potential problems before they grow. For older homes in Provo, proactive pipe replacement might be the best long-term solution.

    Full service line replacement usually requires a plumbing permit and inspection from your city or county. Small spot repairs often do not. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when one is required.

    Most standard water line repairs can be completed within a few hours to a full day, depending on the severity and accessibility of the leak. More complex issues, like trenchless pipe replacement or extensive excavation for a main line, might take 1-3 days. We always aim for the quickest, most efficient fix without compromising quality.
