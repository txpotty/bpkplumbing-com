    Kitchen Sink Drain Replacement in Provo

    Kitchen drains start failing at the basket strainer. Old strainer putty dries out and the locknut rusts, so water runs down the tailpiece and looks like a trap leak. Strainer and trap get swapped as a pair. Disposal sinks get a new flange instead.

    Pop-Up Drain Replacement in Provo

    Lift-rod pop-ups are the most common bath drain. We pull the old body, clean the flange seat down to bare porcelain, reset the new flange in putty or silicone, and adjust the clevis so the lift rod opens and closes the stopper with one easy motion. We match the faucet finish. Overflow-style drains are standard.

    Utility Sink Drain Replacement in Provo

    Laundry tubs see lint and paint water. Lint and grit wear out thin utility tub drains and pack the trap so it never drains right. A 1-1/2 inch trap with a cleanout plug goes in. A lint trap on the washer hose helps.

    Pop-Up Drain Assembly Replacement in Provo, UT

    A stopper that drops on its own has a worn pivot. The plastic ball on the pivot rod wears flat, the nut that holds it loses its seal, and the rod no longer holds the stopper up, so the sink either will not drain or will not hold water. One new pop-up fixes every part of that. Takes about an hour.

    Corroded Drain Replacement in Provo, UT

    Drain cleaners speed up corrosion. Caustic products sit in the bottom of a metal trap between uses and eat at the chrome plating and the thin brass underneath, which is why so many old traps we pull have pinholes right at the bottom of the bend. PVC does not have that problem. We recommend it for most cabinets.
