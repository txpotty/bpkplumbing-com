    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Replacement in Provo

    Costs vary by job. A tank water heater replacement usually runs $1,400 to $3,000, a water softener $1,500 to $3,500, a water line $1,800 to $5,500 and a sewer line $4,000 to $15,000 or more.

    The timeline depends on the complexity of the job. A water heater replacement typically takes 2 to 4 hours. A major project like a full house repipe or sewer line replacement can take 1 to 5 days. We will provide an estimated schedule during your consultation.

    Key signs include frequent, recurring leaks in the same area, discolored water, very low water pressure throughout the house, heavy corrosion visible on pipes or a major component like a water heater being over 10 to 15 years old and failing. If repair costs approach the cost of replacement, replacement is often the smarter long-term investment.

    Replacing old pipes with PEX or copper offers numerous benefits. PEX is flexible, corrosion-resistant and less prone to bursting in freezes, while copper is durable and long-lasting. Both improve water quality, eliminate leaks from old pipes and can significantly boost water pressure and flow throughout your Provo home.

    Equipment comes with manufacturer warranties, often 6 to 12 years for water heaters and longer for some pipe materials. We also guarantee our installation labor.

    We mainly use PEX (cross-linked polyethylene) and copper piping for water supply lines. For sewer and drain lines, we use durable PVC (polyvinyl chloride) or ABS (acrylonitrile butadiene styrene) pipes, based on the application and local codes.

    Frequent leaks, rusty or discolored water, low pressure, recurring backups and equipment over 10 years old are common signs. We can inspect and give you an honest assessment.

    Climate, freezes and soil type all affect how long plumbing lasts. Higher elevations in and around Provo bring colder nights and longer winters, which puts extra load on water heaters and heating systems. Freeze-thaw cycles can damage pipes, and corrosive or shifting soils can speed up pipe degradation. Soils in Utah vary from clay that swells when wet to rocky benches and collapsible soils that settle when they take on water.
