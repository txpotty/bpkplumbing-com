    What We Fix in Provo, UT Homes

    Well Pump Service in Provo, UT

    Submersible pumps sit deep in the casing, so diagnosis happens at the surface first. BPK Plumbing checks the pressure switch, the control box capacitor and the tank precharge, then measures amp draw on the motor. A pump drawing high amps is wearing out. Jet pumps are simpler, and most of the time a bad foot valve or a lost prime is to blame.

    Slow Drain Repair in Provo

    Gurgling toilets and a tub that drains slow often trace to a plugged roof vent or a sagging drain line. After clearing it, we camera the line and fix whatever is broken. Common fixes include replacing corroded cast iron, cutting out failed ABS joints, rebuilding a broken trap arm and resetting toilets on a new wax ring and flange. The goal is a drain you stop thinking about.

    Well Pump Repair in Provo, UT

    No water at the tap on a well system narrows the list quickly. It is usually the pressure switch, the capacitor in the control box, the pump motor or a waterlogged pressure tank. Voltage and amp readings at the control box tell us whether the pump needs to come up. Pulling a submersible pump is a two-person job.

    Drain Line Repair in Provo

    Slow drains that come back right after a snake usually mean the pipe itself is damaged. Cast iron that has cracked or a run that sags will hold debris no matter how often it is cleared. We run a camera to find the spot, then replace that section or hydro-jet the line if buildup is the only problem. Grease is the usual buildup.

    Tank and Tankless Water Heater Repair in Provo

    Tank and tankless heaters fail in different ways. BPK Plumbing repairs both, across all the major brands, gas and electric. On a tank we check the thermocouple, gas valve, elements and anode rod. Tankless trouble is usually scale in the heat exchanger or a dirty inlet screen, and a descaling flush fixes more of them than new parts do.

    Gas Leak Repair in Provo

    Rotten-egg odor near the range or a hiss at the meter means get out of the house first. Phone the gas company from the yard, then call us to fix it. We track the leak with a combustible gas detector and bubble solution, then replace whatever failed and pressure test the line. No shortcuts on gas.
