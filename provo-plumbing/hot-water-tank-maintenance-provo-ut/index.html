    Rheem and Bradford White Tank Service in Provo

    Rheem, Bradford White, AO Smith and State make most of the tanks in local garages, and each has quirks a tech should know before opening a drain valve. On some models the anode sits under a plastic top cap. Bradford White often uses a combo anode in the hot outlet nipple. We carry the right sockets for each brand.

    Commercial Tank Maintenance in Provo, UT

    Restaurants, salons, laundromats and small offices run tanks much harder than a household does, often 75 to 100 gallons cycling all day. Sediment builds up two or three times faster. Most commercial tanks go on a quarterly or twice-yearly schedule with a dated log on the jacket. Downtime gets scheduled outside business hours.

    Standard Tank Tune-Up in Provo

    A standard tune-up starts with the fuel off and the cold supply closed, then a full drain through the bottom valve until the water runs clear. That alone takes 30 to 40 minutes. We check the heating elements or burner, read the thermostat setting and look at every fitting on top of the tank for green crust or drips. Small leaks at the dielectric nipples get noted on your report.

    Electric Thermostat Service in Provo

    Each element on an electric tank has its own thermostat, and the upper one carries a reset button for the high-limit switch. When the reset trips, something caused it. We find out why it tripped, which usually comes down to a shorted element or a thermostat stuck closed, before we press it back in. Power stays off at the breaker the whole time.

    Electric Element Check in Provo

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. We test each one with a meter. A failed lower element shows up as hot water that runs out halfway through a shower. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    Anode Upgrades for Odor in Provo, UT

    A rotten egg smell from the hot side only is almost always sulfate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. A different rod usually solves it. We replace it with an aluminum-zinc rod or a powered anode, then chlorinate the tank to kill the bacteria living in it. Powered anodes never wear out and draw very little electricity.

    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Provo

    Calcium carbonate drops out of hard water every time the tank heats, and over a year it can harden into chunks the size of a thumbnail. That is what makes the popping noise. For stubborn scale we use a potable-rated descaler, then flush it clean. A clean bottom puts less heat stress on the seam.

    Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Provo

    Street pressure over 80 psi is hard on every fixture in the house, and plumbing codes generally call for a pressure reducing valve above that number. We check static pressure at a hose bib. The relief valve lever gets lifted next. If the valve opens and reseats cleanly and the expansion tank holds its charge, the tank is safe from pressure spikes.
