    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Leak Repair in Provo

    Most wet basement repairs fall between $500 and $5,000, and the spread comes from what is causing the water. Downspout and grading fixes cost the least. Interior drains cost more since the slab gets cut, and exterior work costs most because of the digging. You get the price in writing before work starts.

    Look for damp spots on walls or floors, a musty smell, and white chalky deposits on concrete. Those deposits are efflorescence, left behind when water evaporates. Peeling paint low on the wall, rust on the water heater base, and a water bill that keeps creeping up with no change in use all point toward a leak worth testing. Smell often comes first.

    Only some repairs need excavation. Exterior waterproofing and footing drain replacement mean digging down to the footing along the leaking wall. Pipe repairs, crack injection and interior drain systems are all done from inside the basement, which is why they are the more common choice on homes with patios, driveways or landscaping right against the foundation.

    Musty odors and damp carpet are the early ones. Then come peeling paint and white mineral lines. A water bill that climbs or a sump that runs more than usual are worth a call too.

    Crack repairs use two-part polyurethane or epoxy injection resin, depending on whether the crack is actively wet. Polyurethane handles wet cracks. Our interior drains use rigid perforated pipe, which keeps its slope and can be jetted later.

    Leak repair stops a specific source, like a cracked pipe or a leaking wall crack. Waterproofing covers the whole wall. It handles groundwater all around the foundation and makes sense when water comes in at many points.

    Gutters and downspouts do most of the work. Clear them and extend the outlets away from the walls. Test the sump each spring and swap rubber washer hoses for braided ones every five years.
