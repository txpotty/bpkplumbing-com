    PEX Water Line Repair in Lehi, UT

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rodents chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and pipe left in the sun before it was buried can get brittle. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    After-Hours Water Line Breaks in Lehi, UT

    Water lines tend to break on a Saturday night. Ours is a 24 hour line. BPK Plumbing sends a plumber who can shut off the curb stop, pump the hole and make a permanent repair on the spot, which saves you from a weekend on a temporary patch. If the break is on the utility side of the meter, we tell you right away and help you get the provider out. It is more common than most people expect.

    Commercial Water Line Repair in Lehi, UT

    Commercial service lines are often 1 1/2 to 4 inch pipe feeding a meter bank, a backflow assembly and sometimes a fire line. A break shuts the business down. BPK Plumbing can schedule repairs early in the morning or after closing, and we bring the larger fittings and couplings on the truck. We also arrange the backflow test and inspection that most water providers require after work on the commercial side of the meter.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Lehi, UT

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Repeated leaks on galvanized pipe call for a new line. Age matters too, and a line past about 40 years is usually near the end no matter the material. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. We show you the pipe we exposed.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Lehi, UT

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. By the time one leaks, the rest of the pipe is close behind. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, which can hold for a while if the pipe is not too far gone. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. That fixes the pressure too.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Lehi

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Older homes in Lehi may still have galvanized steel supply lines that rust inside and cause low pressure and rusty water. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. We pinpoint the leak with acoustic gear and thermal imaging. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. We price each one for you.

    Emergency Water Line Repair in Lehi, UT

    First thing on a burst line is shutting the water off, at the house valve if it works and at the curb stop if it does not. Water pouring into a yard undermines everything around it. BPK Plumbing sends a plumber with a curb key, a pump and repair couplings. Most breaks are shut down quickly. If the repair will run long, a temporary bypass keeps water flowing to the house during the repair.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Lehi

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. With PEX, the fitting is the weak point most of the time. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, while PEX gets a fresh crimp ring or expansion fitting with the right tool for the brand.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Lehi

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. The deeper the pipe, the higher the price. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, and trenchless replacement often lands between $3,000 and $7,000 because of the equipment involved. The written price comes before we start.

    Watch the bill first. An unexplained spike usually comes before anything you can see. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, soggy ground in dry weather, discolored water and a hissing sound near the meter. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    Usually not. The digging is the risky part. Gas, power and fiber can run right alongside a water line, which is why 811 marking and hand digging near those lines matter so much. Joining pipe underground also takes the right fittings and tools, plus a pressure test before backfill.

    Labor is warrantied, always. New pipe and fittings carry the manufacturer warranty as well, and both terms are written on your estimate before any work starts. Keep the invoice. You will want it if anything comes up.

    In most places the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. You own the rest. Some providers draw the line at the curb stop instead, so we confirm where the leak sits before quoting anything. If it turns out to be theirs, we tell you and help you report it.

    You can tighten an exposed fitting at a hose bib. Buried repairs are not a weekend project. They need the leak located, utility marking, careful excavation and burial-rated fittings. A mistake means a bigger leak and the same hole dug twice.

    A full service line replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. Small repairs often do not. The rules come from your city or county, so the answer can change from one town to the next. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when it is required.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, rusty water and a climbing bill are other common clues. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.
